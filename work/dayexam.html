DayExam

An exam-prep platform for SSC, HSC, BCS and admission tests, built for a client with no technical background who handed me the whole decision space.

Client
A teacher with no technical background
My role
Product and UX design, solo
Built
Web, then admin dashboard, then mobile app
Status
Live · 3.5K+ students enrolled
01

The brief

“I only know teaching. You know website, app, and dashboard design — you decide what’s right.”

The client was a teacher, not a technical person. He wanted one thing: a place where students and guardians could log in, buy courses or exams, and take tests. Beyond that, he left research, decisions and testing to me. His only role was to approve or reject at the end.

02

Directing AI, and what I built

I did the market research myself: how Bangladeshi students and guardians behave, what they trust, what puts them off. Then I fed it to AI to cross-check and fill gaps, judging the output as a Bangladeshi user, not just as a designer.

AI’s first flow ideas were wrong. I rewrote the core flow by hand seven times before it held. Then I stopped asking AI for flows and started drawing them myself, handing AI a finished flow and a token system to build from.

Role-flexible accounts

One login moves between guardian and student. A parent can buy a course for their child and separately sit their own exam (BCS, bank job) without two accounts.

bKash and card, both

A fully online business with no cash option. bKash covers most student users, card covers the rest.

Split grading

MCQs auto-score with wrong-answer deductions. Written sections are graded by hand by the assigned teacher. No AI grading, since the client’s budget did not cover it.

Web, dashboard, app

The website first, then a client-requested admin dashboard, then a mobile app once he saw the responsive web version working. One design system across all three.

03

The real problem

“Let people buy and take exams” was hiding three unresolved shapes.

First, the account model. A guardian and a student are not two user types. They are roles one person moves between, sometimes for different exams at different levels.

Second, the payment flow. AI’s version stopped at “select course, pay”. It had no “my exams” view, no result history, and no answer on whether a retake is free. I asked the client directly and got the real business rule: you have already paid once, so a retake is free. I did not guess.

Third, the grading split. Written answers had to go to the same teacher who set the question, given the budget.

04

What I rejected

I rejected AI’s first flow outright and rebuilt it by hand across seven revisions. It kept treating “buy a course” as the end of the flow, not the start of the exam-taking journey.

I also rejected letting AI touch grading, even after the client himself asked whether AI could summarise written answers. There was no budget for the integration, and a wrong auto-grade on a real exam is worse than a slower manual one.

05

Outcome

Live at dayexam.com, on web and on the app. The client was satisfied enough to green-light the dashboard, then the app.

3.5K+students enrolled
3surfaces shipped: web, dashboard, app
7hand-rewritten flow revisions
What I got wrong

My first flow draft ended at “purchase complete”. I had not designed what happens right after someone buys: where they land and how they get into the exam. The external dev team caught it when they tried to build it and asked me directly. I fixed it by documenting the flow properly first, then handing over visual design, tokens and a guideline, across several rounds of meetings with a dev team I did not manage.
